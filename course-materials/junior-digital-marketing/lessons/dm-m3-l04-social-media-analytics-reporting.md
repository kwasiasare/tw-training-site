## Why Social Media Analytics Matter

Posting content without measuring results is guessing. Analytics tell you what your audience actually responds to, whether you are moving toward business goals, and where to spend your time next. As a junior assistant, your job is often to **gather the numbers and present them clearly** so the team can make decisions.

## Core Metrics You Need to Know

- **Reach** — the number of unique accounts that saw your content.
- **Impressions** — the total number of times content was displayed (one person can create several impressions).
- **Engagement** — interactions: likes, comments, shares, saves, clicks.
- **Engagement rate** — engagement relative to reach or followers, expressed as a percentage.
- **Clicks / link clicks** — taps on a link, profile, or call-to-action.
- **Click-through rate (CTR)** — clicks divided by impressions.
- **Follower growth** — net new followers over a period.
- **Conversions** — a desired action completed (sign-up, purchase, download), tracked with UTM links or pixels.
- **Video views / watch time / completion rate** — for video-first platforms.

## Vanity vs Actionable Metrics

A **vanity metric** looks impressive but does not guide decisions on its own (e.g. raw follower count or total impressions). An **actionable metric** ties to a goal and informs next steps (e.g. engagement rate on link posts, CTR to the website, cost per conversion). Always ask: *"So what? What would I do differently because of this number?"*

## Key Formulas

```
Engagement rate (by reach) = (total engagements ÷ reach) × 100
CTR = (link clicks ÷ impressions) × 100
Follower growth rate = (net new followers ÷ starting followers) × 100
Conversion rate = (conversions ÷ clicks) × 100
```

## Where to Find the Data (Native Tools)

- **Instagram** — Professional Dashboard / Insights: reach, interactions, follower demographics, top content.
- **Facebook** — Meta Business Suite Insights: reach, engagement, page views.
- **LinkedIn** — Page Analytics: impressions, engagement rate, follower demographics by job title/industry.
- **TikTok** — Analytics tab: video views, watch time, traffic source, follower activity times.
- **X (Twitter)** — Analytics: impressions, engagements, top tweets.
- **YouTube** — YouTube Studio: views, watch time, audience retention.

Native analytics are free and accurate but only cover one platform at a time. Export to a spreadsheet to compare and track trends over time.

## Building a Clear Performance Report

A good report is short, visual and decision-focused. A reliable structure:

1. **Summary / TL;DR** — 2-3 sentences: what happened this period.
2. **Goals & KPIs** — what you were aiming for and the targets.
3. **Results** — key metrics vs last period and vs goal, with simple charts.
4. **Top performing content** — 2-3 posts and *why* they worked.
5. **Underperformers / learnings** — what flopped and the lesson.
6. **Next steps / recommendations** — 3-5 concrete actions.

### Tips for Reporting
- Always compare to a benchmark: previous period, goal, or industry average. A number alone is meaningless.
- Use percentages and trends (▲ +12%) not just raw totals.
- Round sensibly and keep charts simple.
- Write for a busy reader — lead with the takeaway, then the detail.
- End every report with **what you will do next**.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Reporting impressions without context or comparison.
- Mixing date ranges across platforms.
- Drowning the report in every available metric — pick the ones tied to goals.
- Forgetting to explain *why* something performed well so it can be repeated.
