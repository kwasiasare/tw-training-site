## What Is a Keyword?

A **keyword** is the word or phrase a person types (or speaks) into a search engine. In digital marketing we use keywords to understand what audiences want and to make sure our content and ads appear when they search.

Key terms you must know:

- **Search volume** — roughly how many times a keyword is searched per month.
- **Keyword difficulty (KD)** — how hard it is to rank on page one (higher = more competition).
- **Long-tail keyword** — a longer, more specific phrase (e.g. *"vegan running shoes for flat feet"*). Lower volume but easier to rank and higher intent.
- **Head term** — a short, broad keyword (e.g. *"shoes"*). High volume, very competitive, vague intent.

## Search Intent: The Heart of Modern SEO

Google rewards content that matches **why** someone is searching, not just the words they use. There are four core intent types:

| Intent | What the user wants | Example query |
|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | To learn something | "how to tie a tie" |
| **Navigational** | To reach a specific site/brand | "netflix login" |
| **Commercial** | To research before buying | "best budget laptops 2024" |
| **Transactional** | To take action / buy now | "buy iphone 15 case" |

**Why it matters:** If you write a blog post for a transactional keyword, you'll lose to product pages. Match the content *type* to the intent.

## A Simple Keyword Research Workflow

1. **Seed keywords** — brainstorm 5–10 core topics for the business.
2. **Expand** — feed seeds into tools to get hundreds of related ideas.
3. **Pull data** — note volume and difficulty for each.
4. **Classify intent** — label every keyword I/N/C/T.
5. **Cluster** — group keywords by topic + intent.
6. **Prioritise** — pick targets using value vs. effort.

## Free & Freemium Tools

- **Google autocomplete & "People also ask"** — free idea generators built into search.
- **Google Keyword Planner** — free with a Google Ads account; gives volume ranges and ad competition.
- **Google Trends** — free; shows seasonality and rising terms.
- **AnswerThePublic** — freemium; surfaces question-based keywords (great for informational intent).
- **Ubersuggest / Keyword Surfer / Semrush (free tier)** — freemium; volume, difficulty, and related terms.

## Reading Intent From the SERP

The fastest way to confirm intent: **search the keyword yourself** and look at what ranks.

- Lots of blog posts/guides → informational
- Product or category pages, shopping ads → transactional/commercial
- A brand's homepage → navigational

If the top results are all product pages, don't try to rank a blog post there.

## Prioritising Keywords (Value vs. Effort)

Score each keyword on:

- **Value** — relevance to the business + intent (transactional often = higher value).
- **Effort** — keyword difficulty + how much content you'd need.

Target **low-difficulty, high-relevance** keywords first (quick wins), especially long-tail terms. Save high-volume head terms for long-term campaigns.

## Using Clusters for SEO and Ads

- **SEO:** Build one page per cluster (a "topic"), targeting the main keyword plus supporting long-tails.
- **Ads:** Group keywords into tight ad groups by intent so your ad copy matches the search — improving Quality Score and lowering cost.

## Key Takeaways

- Keywords reveal demand; intent reveals *how to serve it*.
- Always match content type to intent.
- Use free tools first — you can do excellent research for £0.
- Long-tail + low difficulty = best early wins.
