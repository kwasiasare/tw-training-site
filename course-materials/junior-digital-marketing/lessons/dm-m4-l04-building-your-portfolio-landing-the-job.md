## Why a Portfolio Matters for Junior Marketers

Entry-level marketing is competitive, and employers rarely hire on a degree alone. They want **proof you can do the work**: plan a campaign, create content, read analytics, and communicate results. A portfolio turns your bootcamp projects into evidence. Even if your work was a practice or capstone project, presenting it professionally shows initiative and skill.

> Rule of thumb: *Show, don't just tell.* A screenshot of a real email open-rate report beats the phrase "familiar with email marketing."

## What to Include in a Marketing Portfolio

Aim for **3–5 strong pieces** rather than everything you've made. Quality and clarity win.

- **Email campaign** — subject lines, the email design, audience, and the results (open rate, click rate).
- **Social media content** — sample posts, a content calendar, and engagement numbers.
- **Analytics snapshot** — a screenshot or summary showing you can read data (Google Analytics, Meta Insights, Mailchimp reports).
- **A short strategy doc** — goals, target audience, channels, and KPIs for one project.
- **Capstone case study** — your full project tying it all together.

For practice projects, label them clearly (e.g., "Mock campaign for a local coffee shop") so you're honest while still showcasing skill.

## The Case Study Formula (Problem → Action → Result)

Each portfolio piece should tell a quick story:

1. **Context / Problem** — "A small bakery wanted to grow their email list and drive weekend sales."
2. **Goal & Strategy** — "Goal: 200 new subscribers in 4 weeks. Strategy: a 10% discount lead magnet promoted on Instagram."
3. **Action** — what you actually did: wrote the emails, designed the signup form, built the automation.
4. **Result** — the numbers: "32% open rate (industry avg ~21%), 140 new subscribers, 12 redemptions."
5. **Reflection** — what you learned or would improve next time.

Always include **metrics**, and add context so numbers mean something (compare to averages or starting points).

## Where to Host Your Portfolio

You don't need to be a web developer. Use a free or low-cost tool:

- **Notion** — fast, clean, easy to share with a link.
- **Canva** — design a PDF or simple website.
- **Carrd / Wix / Google Sites** — simple one-page sites.
- **PDF** — a polished document you can attach to applications.

Make sure it's **mobile-friendly**, loads fast, and has a clear way to contact you.

## Tailoring Your Resume

Recruiters skim resumes in seconds, and many companies use **Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)** that scan for keywords.

- **Mirror the job description.** If it says "email marketing, content creation, Google Analytics," use those exact terms (truthfully).
- **Lead with achievements, not duties.** Use the formula: *action verb + what you did + result/metric.*
  - Weak: "Responsible for social media posts."
  - Strong: "Created and scheduled 30+ Instagram posts, growing engagement 18% over 6 weeks."
- **Include a skills section** with tools: Mailchimp, Canva, Meta Business Suite, Google Analytics, Hootsuite.
- **Add your portfolio link** at the top near your contact info.
- Keep it to **one page** for entry level.

## Optimize LinkedIn

- Professional headshot and a headline that says what you do: "Junior Digital Marketer | Email & Social Media | Open to work."
- A short **About** summary with your focus and portfolio link.
- Add your bootcamp, projects, and skills. Turn on the **Open to Work** setting.

## Acing the Interview with STAR

Use the **STAR method** for behavioral questions:

- **S**ituation — set the scene briefly.
- **T**ask — what needed to be done.
- **A**ction — what *you* did.
- **R**esult — the outcome, with a metric if possible.

Common junior marketing questions to prepare:

- "Walk me through a campaign you're proud of." (Use your capstone.)
- "How do you measure success on social media?" (Mention KPIs: reach, engagement rate, CTR, conversions.)
- "What marketing tools have you used?"
- "How do you stay current with trends?"

Prepare 2–3 thoughtful questions to ask them, and follow up with a thank-you email within 24 hours.

## Quick Action Plan

1. Pick your 3–5 best pieces.
2. Write each as a Problem→Action→Result case study with metrics.
3. Build a Notion/Canva portfolio and grab the share link.
4. Tailor your resume and LinkedIn to a target job posting.
5. Practice STAR answers out loud.
