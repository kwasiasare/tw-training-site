## Why Meta Ads?

Meta (Facebook + Instagram) reaches billions of users daily and offers granular targeting at low entry cost. As a junior assistant, you'll often be asked to launch and monitor simple campaigns, so mastering Ads Manager is essential.

## The Three-Level Structure

Every Meta campaign uses a strict hierarchy:

1. **Campaign** — where you set the *objective* (what you want to achieve).
2. **Ad Set** — where you define *audience, budget, schedule, and placements*.
3. **Ad** — the actual *creative* (image/video, copy, headline, link, call-to-action).

A single campaign can hold multiple ad sets, and each ad set can hold multiple ads. This lets you test audiences and creatives systematically.

## Step 1 — Set Up Ads Manager

- You need a **Facebook Business Page** and a **Meta Business Suite / Business Manager** account.
- Add a **payment method** under Billing.
- Confirm your Page and ad account permissions before building.

## Step 2 — Choose the Right Objective

Meta groups objectives by funnel stage:

| Objective | Use when you want… |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Maximum reach / brand visibility |
| Traffic | Clicks to a website or app |
| Engagement | Likes, comments, messages, video views |
| Leads | Form fills, sign-ups |
| App promotion | App installs |
| Sales | Conversions / purchases (needs the Meta Pixel) |

**Rule of thumb:** match the objective to the *business goal*, not to vanity metrics. Choosing 'Sales' tells Meta's algorithm to find buyers, not just clickers.

## Step 3 — Define Your Audience

Three audience types you'll use most:

- **Saved (Core) audiences** — built from location, age, gender, interests, and behaviours.
- **Custom audiences** — people who already know you: website visitors (via Pixel), customer email lists, video viewers, page engagers.
- **Lookalike audiences** — Meta finds new people similar to a custom audience (e.g., 1% lookalike of your best customers).

Keep audiences broad enough to deliver (avoid over-narrowing) but relevant. Watch the **audience size estimate** indicator.

## Step 4 — Budget & Schedule

- **Daily budget**: average amount spent per day — good for ongoing campaigns.
- **Lifetime budget**: total spend across the whole run — good for fixed promotions; allows ad scheduling (dayparting).
- Start small (e.g., £5–£10/day) to gather data before scaling.
- Meta uses an **auction**; you usually let it bid automatically (Highest Volume) when starting out.

## Step 5 — Placements

- **Advantage+ placements (automatic)** is recommended for beginners — Meta distributes across Feeds, Stories, Reels, etc.
- Use **Manual placements** only when you have a specific reason (e.g., Instagram-only creative).

## Step 6 — Build the Ad Creative

- Choose a format: single image, video, or carousel.
- Write clear **primary text**, a punchy **headline**, and a relevant **call-to-action** button.
- Use a strong destination URL and add **UTM parameters** for tracking.
- Follow image specs (e.g., 1080×1080 square or 1080×1350 vertical for feeds/Reels).

## Step 7 — Review, Publish, Monitor

- Check Ads Manager warnings, then click **Publish**. Ads enter **review** (usually within 24 hours).
- After launch, watch: **Reach, Impressions, CPM, CTR, CPC, Results, and Cost per Result**.
- Let campaigns exit the **learning phase** (~50 conversions/week) before judging performance; avoid editing too soon.

## Policy & Compliance Notes

- Avoid prohibited content and respect ad policies (no misleading claims, restricted categories).
- Sensitive categories (housing, employment, credit) require **Special Ad Categories** with limited targeting.

## Quick Launch Checklist

1. Objective matches goal
2. Audience defined and sized
3. Budget set (start small)
4. Placements chosen
5. Creative + copy + CTA + URL ready
6. Tracking (Pixel/UTMs) in place
7. Review warnings → Publish
