## What Is On-Page SEO?

**On-page SEO** is the practice of optimizing individual web pages—both the content and the HTML source code—so they rank higher in search engines and earn more relevant traffic. It's different from:

- **Off-page SEO** — actions taken *outside* your site (backlinks, mentions, social signals).
- **Technical SEO** — site speed, crawlability, mobile-friendliness, structured data.

As a Junior Social Media / Digital Marketing Assistant, on-page SEO is the part you can most directly influence when you create or edit content.

## Why It Matters

Search engines need to understand *what your page is about* and *who it's for*. On-page signals help them do that. Good on-page SEO also improves the **user experience** and **click-through rate (CTR)** from the search results page (SERP).

## The Core On-Page Elements

### 1. Title Tag
The clickable headline shown in search results and browser tabs.

- Keep it roughly **50–60 characters** so it doesn't get cut off.
- Put the **primary keyword near the front**.
- Make it compelling and unique for every page.
- Add your brand at the end when there's room.

```html
<title>Beginner's Guide to On-Page SEO | Acme Marketing</title>
```

### 2. Meta Description
A short summary (not a direct ranking factor, but it drives clicks).

- Aim for **120–155 characters**.
- Include the keyword and a clear benefit or call-to-action.
- Write it like an ad: make people want to click.

```html
<meta name="description" content="Learn on-page SEO basics: optimize titles, meta descriptions, headings, and content to rank higher. A practical checklist for beginners.">
```

### 3. Headings (H1–H6)
Headings structure content for readers and search engines.

- Use **one H1** per page (usually the main page heading/topic).
- Use **H2s** for main sections and **H3s** for sub-points.
- Include keywords naturally—don't force them.

### 4. URL Slug
- Keep URLs short, lowercase, and readable.
- Separate words with hyphens.
- Include the keyword; avoid dates, IDs, and stop words where possible.
- Good: `/blog/on-page-seo-guide` — Bad: `/blog/p?id=4821&cat=12`

### 5. Content & Keywords
- Start with **keyword research**: find terms your audience searches for (use Google autocomplete, 'People also ask', or a keyword tool).
- Match **search intent** — is the searcher looking to learn, compare, or buy?
- Use the primary keyword in the title, H1, first 100 words, and naturally throughout.
- Add related/secondary keywords and synonyms.
- **Avoid keyword stuffing** — write for humans first.

### 6. Images
- Use descriptive **file names** (`on-page-seo-checklist.jpg`, not `IMG_2931.jpg`).
- Add **alt text** describing the image (helps accessibility and image search).
- Compress images so they load fast.

### 7. Internal Links
- Link to other relevant pages on your site using **descriptive anchor text**.
- Helps users navigate and spreads ranking value across pages.

## A Simple On-Page SEO Workflow

1. Pick **one primary keyword** per page.
2. Write a unique **title tag** (~55 chars, keyword near front).
3. Write a click-worthy **meta description** (~150 chars).
4. Set a clear **H1** and logical H2/H3 structure.
5. Create a clean **URL slug**.
6. Write helpful content that satisfies search intent.
7. Optimize **images** (file names + alt text).
8. Add **internal links** with descriptive anchors.
9. Review on mobile and proofread.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Duplicate or missing title tags.
- Stuffing keywords unnaturally.
- Multiple H1s or skipping heading levels for styling.
- Thin content that doesn't answer the query.
- Generic alt text like 'image1'.

## Key Takeaway

On-page SEO is about clearly communicating your page's topic to both **search engines and people**. Master the checklist and apply it every time you publish or edit a page.
