## Why Copywriting Matters

Great design gets noticed, but words make people *act*. Copywriting is writing with a goal: getting the reader to click, sign up, buy, or share. On social media and ads you have seconds to earn attention, so every word counts.

Good marketing copy is **clear before clever**. It speaks to one person, focuses on benefits (what the reader gains), and ends with a single obvious next step.

## The AIDA Framework

AIDA maps the journey from stranger to customer:

- **Attention** — a hook that stops the scroll (bold claim, question, surprising stat).
- **Interest** — build relevance: why should they keep reading?
- **Desire** — show the benefit and outcome; make them *want* it.
- **Action** — tell them exactly what to do next.

*Example (skincare ad):*
> **Attention:** Tired of breakouts that won't quit?
> **Interest:** Our serum targets the root cause, not just the surface.
> **Desire:** Clearer skin in 14 days — backed by 2,000+ five-star reviews.
> **Action:** Tap to get 20% off your first bottle.

## The PAS Framework

PAS is fast and emotional — great for ads and short captions:

- **Problem** — name the pain the reader feels.
- **Agitate** — stir it up; remind them why it's frustrating.
- **Solution** — present your product as the relief.

*Example (productivity app):*
> **Problem:** Your to-do list never seems to shrink.
> **Agitate:** You end each day exhausted but still behind.
> **Solution:** TaskFlow plans your day automatically — finish by 5pm.

## Writing Scroll-Stopping Hooks

The first line decides if anyone reads the rest. Strong hooks:

- Ask a question your audience says "yes" to.
- Lead with a number or surprising stat.
- Make a bold, specific promise.
- Call out the audience directly ("Small business owners:").
- Tease a result or transformation.

Avoid weak openers like "We are excited to announce..." — nobody scrolls for that.

## Benefits vs. Features

Features describe the product; benefits describe what the customer *gets*.

- Feature: "500ml insulated bottle."
- Benefit: "Cold drinks all day, even at the beach."

Rule of thumb: after every feature, ask **"so what?"** to find the benefit.

## Calls-to-Action (CTAs)

A CTA tells the reader exactly what to do. Strong CTAs are:

- **Action-led:** start with a verb (Shop, Download, Book, Try).
- **Specific:** "Get your free trial" beats "Click here."
- **Low-friction:** reduce risk ("No card needed").
- **Singular:** one CTA per post or ad.

## Adapting to Each Platform

- **Instagram:** strong first line (only ~125 chars show before "more"); friendly tone; emojis okay.
- **LinkedIn:** professional, value-driven, more credibility/data.
- **Facebook/Meta Ads:** short headline + benefit + clear CTA; match the visual.
- **X/Twitter:** punchy, one idea per post.

## Editing Checklist

Before you publish, tighten your copy:

1. Cut filler words (just, really, very, in order to).
2. Read the first line alone — does it hook?
3. One benefit clearly stated?
4. One clear CTA?
5. Read it aloud — does it sound human?
