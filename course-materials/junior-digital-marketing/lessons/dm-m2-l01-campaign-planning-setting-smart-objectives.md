## Why plan before you spend?

Paid advertising is the one area of marketing where mistakes cost real money immediately. A poorly planned campaign burns budget on the wrong people, with the wrong message, chasing a goal nobody agreed on. A **campaign brief** is the cheap insurance against that: a single document everyone signs off on before a cent is spent.

A good brief answers five questions:

1. **Why** are we running this? (the business goal)
2. **Who** are we trying to reach? (the audience)
3. **Where** will we reach them? (the channels)
4. **How much** can we spend, and over what period? (budget & timeline)
5. **What** does success look like, in numbers? (the SMART objective)

## The SMART framework

Vague goals like "get more sales" or "grow our following" can never be judged a success or failure. SMART turns them into something you can measure.

- **S — Specific:** What exactly do you want? (e.g. newsletter sign-ups, not "engagement")
- **M — Measurable:** Attach a number you can track in the ad platform or analytics.
- **A — Achievable:** Realistic given the budget, audience size, and timeframe.
- **R — Relevant:** Tied to a real business outcome, not a vanity metric.
- **T — Time-bound:** A clear deadline or campaign window.

**Weak goal:** "Get more website visitors."

**SMART goal:** "Drive 1,500 new website visitors from Australia aged 25–40 to our spring sale page within 30 days, at a cost per click under $0.80, for a total spend of $1,000."

## Defining your audience

Knowing who you're targeting shapes every other decision. Build your audience from three angles:

- **Demographics:** age, gender, location, language, job title.
- **Interests & behaviours:** hobbies, pages they follow, purchase behaviour (Meta excels here).
- **Intent:** what they're actively searching for (Google Search excels here).

A quick **audience persona** helps: give them a name, a problem, and a reason to click. "Budget-conscious Bianca, 32, searching 'cheap running shoes Melbourne'."

## Choosing channels: Meta vs Google

- **Google Search Ads** capture *existing demand*—people already searching for what you offer. Great for high-intent, bottom-of-funnel goals like purchases and leads.
- **Meta Ads (Facebook/Instagram)** *create demand*—they interrupt people based on interests. Great for awareness, discovery, and visually appealing products.
- Many campaigns use both: Meta to build awareness, Google to capture the resulting searches.

## Matching goals to platform objectives

Both platforms make you pick a campaign objective up front, and the algorithm optimises around it. Choose wrong and you waste budget.

- **Awareness / Reach** → top of funnel (Meta Awareness, Google Display/Video).
- **Traffic / Consideration** → mid funnel (Meta Traffic or Engagement, Google Search clicks).
- **Leads / Sales / Conversions** → bottom of funnel (Meta Sales, Google Performance Max or Search with conversions).

## Setting a budget

- Start with the total you can afford, then work backwards: `budget ÷ target cost-per-result = expected results`.
- Distinguish **daily budget** (spend per day) from **lifetime budget** (total over the campaign).
- Always leave room for a **testing phase**—the first few days teach the algorithm and you.

## The campaign brief template

Put it all on one page:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Campaign name | Spring Sale 2025 |
| Business goal | Increase online sales |
| SMART objective | 50 purchases in 30 days at ≤$15 cost-per-purchase |
| Audience | Women 25–45, Australia, interested in fitness |
| Channels | Meta (Instagram), Google Search |
| Budget | $750 total ($25/day) |
| Timeline | 1–30 September |
| Key message | 20% off all activewear |
| Success metric | Cost per purchase, total revenue (ROAS) |

Get this signed off, and you're ready to build the campaign with confidence.
