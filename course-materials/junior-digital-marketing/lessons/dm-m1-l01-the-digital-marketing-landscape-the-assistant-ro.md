## Why this lesson matters

Before you publish a single post, you need a mental map of the field you're entering. Digital marketing can feel like an alphabet soup of channels, tools, and acronyms. This lesson gives you the big picture so that when a manager says "schedule the organic posts and pull the engagement report," you know exactly what they mean and where it fits.

## The digital marketing landscape

Digital marketing is simply promoting products, services, or a brand using digital channels. The easiest way to organise these channels is the **owned / earned / paid** framework:

- **Owned media** — channels you control: your website, blog, email list, and social media profiles. Free to use, but you do the work.
- **Earned media** — exposure you didn't pay for: shares, reviews, press mentions, word of mouth, user-generated content.
- **Paid media** — anything you pay to place: Google Ads, paid social ads, influencer partnerships, sponsored content.

Most campaigns blend all three. A typical mix of **core channels** includes:

- **Social media** (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook, X) — community, brand awareness, engagement
- **Search engine optimisation (SEO)** — getting found organically on Google
- **Search/display advertising (SEM/PPC)** — paid visibility on search and websites
- **Email marketing** — direct, owned communication with subscribers
- **Content marketing** — blogs, videos, guides that attract and educate

## How a campaign flows

Work moves through predictable stages, and the assistant touches almost every one:

1. **Strategy** — goals, audience, budget, key messages (usually set by a manager)
2. **Planning** — content calendar, channel choices, deadlines
3. **Creation** — copy, graphics, short videos
4. **Scheduling & publishing** — getting content live at the right time
5. **Engagement** — replying to comments and messages (community management)
6. **Measurement** — tracking metrics and reporting results
7. **Optimisation** — using what you learned to improve the next round

The assistant is the engine room for stages 2–6: you keep the calendar moving, produce and schedule content, manage the community, and gather the numbers.

## The assistant role: daily responsibilities

A realistic day might include:

- Drafting and scheduling social posts across platforms
- Designing simple graphics in Canva and resizing for each platform
- Writing short captions with relevant hashtags
- Responding to comments and DMs in a friendly, on-brand voice
- Monitoring mentions and flagging anything that needs a manager
- Updating the content calendar and chasing approvals
- Pulling weekly performance numbers into a simple report

You are not usually setting the strategy yet — you are executing it reliably and feeding insights back up.

## Tools you'll actually use

- **Scheduling:** Meta Business Suite, Buffer, Hootsuite, Later
- **Design:** Canva, CapCut (for short video)
- **Analytics:** native platform insights, Google Analytics, simple spreadsheets
- **Collaboration:** Google Workspace / Microsoft 365, Trello or Asana, Slack

## Metrics in plain language

- **Reach / Impressions** — how many people saw it / how many times it was shown
- **Engagement** — likes, comments, shares, saves (interaction)
- **CTR (click-through rate)** — % of viewers who clicked a link
- **Conversions** — people who completed a goal (sign-up, purchase)

Knowing these four lets you read most beginner reports confidently.

## Key takeaway

You are the reliable executor who turns strategy into live, on-brand content and brings back the numbers. Master the map, the tools, and the daily rhythm, and you become indispensable on any marketing team.
