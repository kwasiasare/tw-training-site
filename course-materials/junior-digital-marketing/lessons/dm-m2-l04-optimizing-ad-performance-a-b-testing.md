## Why optimization matters

Launching an ad is only the start. The real job of a Social Media Assistant is to **monitor, test, and improve** so the same budget delivers more results. Optimization is about lowering your **cost-per-result** (CPR) — the cost of each click, lead, or sale — while keeping quality high.

## The metrics that matter

Don't drown in numbers. Focus on metrics tied to your objective.

- **Impressions** — how many times the ad was shown.
- **CTR (Click-Through Rate)** — clicks ÷ impressions. Signals whether the creative/offer grabs attention. Benchmark: ~1%+ on Meta, ~2%+ on Google Search.
- **CPC (Cost Per Click)** — what you pay per click. Lower is better if click quality holds.
- **CPM (Cost Per Mille)** — cost per 1,000 impressions. Reflects audience competition and ad quality.
- **Conversion Rate** — conversions ÷ clicks. Signals whether the landing page/offer delivers.
- **CPR / CPA (Cost Per Result / Acquisition)** — the bottom-line efficiency metric.
- **ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)** — revenue ÷ spend. Key for e-commerce.

### Reading the signals

| Symptom | Likely cause | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low CTR | Weak creative or wrong audience | Test new hook/image |
| High CTR but low conversions | Landing page or offer mismatch | Improve page/offer |
| Rising CPM | Audience too narrow or ad fatigue | Refresh creative, widen audience |
| High CPR overall | Inefficient targeting or bidding | Restructure or A/B test |

## What is A/B testing?

A/B testing (split testing) compares **two versions that differ by ONE variable** to see which performs better. Changing one thing at a time is what makes the result trustworthy — if you change the image *and* the audience, you won't know which drove the change.

### The A/B testing process

1. **Form a hypothesis** — e.g., "A video ad will get a lower cost-per-lead than a static image."
2. **Choose ONE variable** — creative, headline, audience, placement, or CTA.
3. **Define the success metric** — usually CPR or conversion rate, not just CTR.
4. **Split the audience evenly** so each version gets fair, non-overlapping exposure.
5. **Run long enough** — let it gather data (often 4–7 days) and reach enough conversions before judging.
6. **Pick a winner with confidence**, then scale it and test the next variable.

## Running tests on the platforms

- **Meta Ads**: Use the **A/B Test tool** (Experiments) in Ads Manager. It splits audiences so they don't overlap and declares a winner with a confidence level. You can test creative, audience, placement, or delivery optimization.
- **Google Ads**: Use **Experiments** (Drafts & Experiments) for Search/Display, or test ad variations within an ad group. Responsive Search Ads also auto-test asset combinations.

## Avoiding common mistakes

- **Don't judge too early.** Early data is noisy; wait for volume and exit the learning phase.
- **Don't change multiple variables at once.**
- **Don't optimize for vanity metrics.** A high CTR that doesn't convert wastes money.
- **Beware tiny sample sizes** — a 'winner' from 8 clicks isn't real.
- **Account for the learning phase** on Meta (roughly 50 conversions per ad set).

## A weekly optimization routine

1. Check spend pacing and CPR vs. target.
2. Pause clearly losing ads (enough data, far worse CPR).
3. Scale winners gradually (raise budget ~20% at a time).
4. Launch the next A/B test from your backlog of ideas.
5. Log learnings so you build a library of what works.
