## From Body of Work to Offers

You now have a case study, a portfolio, and an ATS-clean resume. The work is real, but most strong career-changers lose not on capability but on **visibility**, no recruiter ever sees the work, or the profile they do see says nothing. This lesson builds the distribution layer: a LinkedIn profile that argues, in thirty seconds, that you can do an AI PM role, a deliberate search that puts that argument in front of the right people, outreach that earns replies, and interview preparation that converts screens into offers. Treat it as a pipeline you engineer and keep full.

## Rewrite Your Profile as an AI PM

**Headline.** This is the most-searched, most-skimmed field on LinkedIn. Name the role you want, your edge, and the key skills: *AI Product Manager | LLM features, evals & metrics | PRDs, roadmapping, A/B testing*. It is keyword-dense so you surface in recruiter searches, and legible so a human reads on. Do not leave it as an unrelated current title.

**About.** Open with one positioning line, then prove it with your capstone and portfolio in specific, quantified language, then name the target role and point at the evidence. Aim for the reader to finish the first two sentences already believing you do this work. The short-answer question in this lesson is exactly that exercise, write yours.

**Featured.** This is your portfolio strip and the single highest-leverage section for a career-changer. Link your portfolio home, the capstone case study, and one or two of your best PRDs or eval write-ups. A reviewer who clicks Featured and sees a clean case study with a metric tree and an honest retro is most of the way to inviting you to a screen.

**Experience and Projects.** Use the Projects section to list your three-to-five artifacts with the same structure as your resume: a one-line problem, the approach, and a quantified result, each linking to the portfolio. Self-directed projects are legitimate experience; present them as such, labeled honestly.

**Skills.** Add the skills that match real AI PM postings, product management, LLMs, evals, prompt design, roadmapping, A/B testing, metrics, Responsible AI, and pin the most important. Skills drive recruiter search and the 'how you match' panel on applications.

**Open to Work.** Turn it on (recruiters-only visibility if you are employed), set the target titles and locations, and you immediately appear in the dedicated recruiter pool.

## Target the Right Titles

The same skill set is posted under several names. Search and set alerts for **AI Product Manager**, **Product Manager - AI/ML**, **Technical Product Manager**, **Product Manager (Machine Learning)**, and **GenAI / LLM Product Manager**. Read ten real postings for your target title and note the recurring tools and phrases, that vocabulary is what you mirror, truthfully, in your profile and resume. If most postings say 'evaluation frameworks' and 'experimentation,' those exact terms belong in your profile because you have done them.

## Mirror the Job Description (Honestly)

Both LinkedIn Recruiter searches and applicant tracking systems rank you on keyword relevance. For each application, align your profile and resume language to the posting's real terms, only for skills you genuinely have and can defend. If a JD emphasizes 'north-star metrics' and you built a metric tree, use that phrase. If it lists something you have never touched, that is a gap to learn, not a word to fake. It is alignment, not invention.

## Outreach That Gets Replies

Applications alone are a low-yield channel; warm outreach multiplies your odds. Keep messages short, specific, and respectful:

- **To a recruiter on a role:** 'Hi NAME, I saw the AI Product Manager opening on TEAM. I recently shipped a portfolio case study, an LLM support assistant with a full eval plan and metric tree (linked in my profile), and I work problem-first on AI features. Would you be open to a quick chat about fit?'
- **To a PM on the team:** reference something real, a post they wrote, a product they shipped, then a one-line fit statement and a small ask ('any advice on what your team values in an AI PM hire?').

Never send identical boilerplate to hundreds of people; it reads as spam and burns your name. Ten specific messages beat two hundred generic ones.

Follow-up is part of the craft. If a message goes unanswered, a single short, polite nudge after a week is reasonable; beyond that, move on without taking it personally, since non-response is usually about the recipient's inbox, not you. When someone does reply, make it easy to help you: be specific about what you are looking for, keep the ask small, and always thank people who make an introduction. The relationships you build here outlast any single application, so treat every interaction as the start of a network rather than a transaction.

## Prepare for the PM Interview

AI PM interviews probe four pillars. Prepare a structured approach for each so you answer with judgment, not improvisation.

- **Product sense.** Problem framing and product design: pick a user, articulate the job-to-be-done, generate and prioritize solutions, and define what success looks like. Practice 'design an AI feature for X' out loud.
- **Execution.** Prioritization and trade-offs: walk a RICE or impact-versus-effort call, sequence a roadmap, and reason about scope cuts. Use your roadmap artifact as a worked example.
- **AI judgement.** When to use AI versus rules, how you evaluate quality (golden sets, LLM-as-judge, online tests), how you design guardrails and human-in-the-loop fallbacks, and how you reason about cost, latency, hallucination, bias, and Responsible AI and the EU AI Act. This is where your case study shines, walk it as a lived example.
- **Metrics.** Define success: choose a north-star metric, decompose it into inputs, design an A/B test, and separate product metrics from model metrics. Be ready to read your metric tree aloud.

Across all four, answer with the same arc the course taught: **intent, decision, trade-offs**, and name the alternative you rejected. That is the clearest signal of product seniority.

## Run a Weekly Loop

Momentum comes from a routine, not a sprint. A workable weekly loop: apply to five to ten well-matched roles with tailored language; send five to ten specific outreach messages; publish or comment once on an AI product topic (a short post on your eval approach keeps you visible and signals expertise); and improve one artifact so your Featured section keeps getting stronger. Track applications in a simple sheet, role, company, date, contact, status, so you follow up deliberately. The search is a pipeline; keep it full and keep moving candidates through it.

Protect against the two failure modes of a job search: burnout from sprinting, and drift from inconsistency. A fixed, modest weekly target you actually hit every week beats a frantic week followed by three idle ones, because consistency is what keeps the pipeline full enough that something is always advancing. Review the sheet weekly to spot where candidates stall, too few first replies points at your outreach or profile; replies but no screens points at your resume; screens but no offers points at interview prep, and fix that specific stage rather than simply doing more of everything.

## The Deliverable

By the end of this lesson you should have a rewritten profile (headline, About, Featured with your case study and portfolio linked, Projects entries, pinned skills, Open to Work on), a shortlist of target titles with saved searches, a keyword-aligned pass on your top postings, a handful of specific outreach messages sent, a prep plan for the four interview pillars, and a weekly routine on the calendar. That is the machine that turns your body of work into an AI PM offer.
