## Why behavioural interviews exist

Most AI and data roles include a **behavioural** (sometimes called *competency*) interview alongside the technical rounds. The core belief is simple: **past behaviour predicts future behaviour**. If you handled a tight deadline well before, you'll probably handle one well again.

These rounds test the things a coding test cannot: communication, ownership, teamwork, dealing with failure, and how you learn. For a maths graduate moving into AI from an L1 support role, this is great news — your support experience is full of exactly these stories.

## The STAR method

**STAR** gives every answer a clear shape so you don't ramble:

- **Situation** — set the scene in one or two sentences.
- **Task** — what was the goal or your responsibility?
- **Action** — what *you* did, step by step. This is the heart of it.
- **Result** — the outcome, ideally with a number, plus what you learned.

Keep Situation and Task short. Spend most of your words on **Action** and finish with a concrete **Result**.

## Build a story bank

Don't memorise scripts — prepare **raw material** you can adapt live. Aim for six to eight stories that, between them, cover the common themes: a success, a failure, a conflict, a deadline, learning something fast, and helping a customer or teammate.

Store them as structured notes so you can rehearse:

```python
story = {
    "theme": "learning fast",
    "situation": "L1 support; new ticketing tool with no docs",
    "task": "Cut my average resolution time within two weeks",
    "action": "Read the API logs, built a SQL cheat-sheet, paired with a senior",
    "result": "Resolution time dropped ~30%; I wrote the team's first runbook",
}
print(story["theme"], "->", story["result"])
```

## Quantify and own the 'I'

Vague results are forgettable. Wherever you can, attach a **number**: percentage faster, hours saved, tickets handled, accuracy gained. If you have no metric, use a clear before-and-after.

And watch your pronouns. Teams matter, but the interviewer is hiring **you**. Use *we* for context and *I* for the actions and decisions you personally owned.

## Your narrative thread

Your 'tell me about yourself' answer should be a **60-second story**, not your CV read aloud. Tie three things together: where you came from (maths), what you do now (support + self-taught AI), and where you're heading (this role).

```bash
# A simple structure to rehearse out loud
# Past:    "I have a maths degree and I love turning messy problems into clear models."
# Present: "In L1 support I learned to debug fast and explain things simply."
# Future:  "I have spent 30+ hours a week building AI projects and want to do it full time."
```

## Your task

Pick three of the common themes, write a STAR story for each, and **say them out loud and timed** — aim for 90 seconds each. Record yourself once; you'll hear the rambling instantly.
